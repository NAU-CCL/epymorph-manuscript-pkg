\section{Survey of epidemic modeling software}\label{sec:S1}

This section supplements the software survey described in the Methods. It gives the full
search protocol and its results (\S\ref{sec:S1-search}), the criterion behind each row of
Table~1 and the borderline calls (\S\ref{sec:S1-criteria}), a summary of every package the
survey identified (\S\ref{sec:S1-summaries}), and the survey's limitations
(\S\ref{sec:S1-limitations}). Package capabilities were verified against primary
documentation as of September 2026. Every count reported here is regenerated from the archived
search exports by the code in the manuscript repository.

\subsection{Search protocol and results}\label{sec:S1-search}

We describe this as a structured, reproducible search rather than a PRISMA systematic review:
the aim is an auditable, repeatable procedure proportionate to a survey of software, not a
formal evidence synthesis. A systematic review of spatial epidemic modeling \emph{studies} is
reported separately~\citep{oshinubi_systematic_2026}.

\textbf{Sources.} We used two kinds of source. \emph{Discovery sources} were browsed or
followed to completion: curated software registries (the CRAN Task View on Epidemiology,
rOpenSci, Bioconductor, PyPI and the Journal of Open Source Software), Google Scholar, a
GitHub topic search (\texttt{epidemics}, \texttt{particle-filter},
\texttt{data-assimilation}) to catch packages outside PyPI and CRAN, and backward and forward
citation chaining from three anchor papers (pomp~\citep{king_statistical_2016},
spatPomp~\citep{asfaw_tutorial_2024}, FlepiMoP~\citep{lemaitre_flepimop_2024}). These have no
meaningful denominator, so we report the packages they surfaced rather than a record count
(Table~\ref{tab:S1-sources}). \emph{Counted sources} were two bibliographic databases, PubMed
and Lens.org, whose result sets were exported in full and screened under one procedure. We
chose them over Scopus or Web of Science because software is often announced in venues the
subscription indexes cover poorly: Lens.org indexes preprint servers, the Journal of Open
Source Software and conference proceedings alongside journal articles, and both databases
permit a full, scriptable export.

\textbf{Search window and string.} The database search is restricted to records from 2020
onward, since its purpose is to surface recently released packages. Packages found through the
discovery sources are eligible regardless of publication year, which keeps established
comparators such as pomp (2016) and SimInf (2019) in scope. The query combines three concept
blocks as \texttt{A AND B AND C}, with terms within each block joined by \texttt{OR}. On
Lens.org it was applied to title, abstract, keyword and field of study; on PubMed to all
fields.

\begin{itemize}\raggedright
\item
  \textbf{A (domain):}
  \texttt{epidemic OR epidemiological OR "infectious disease" OR "disease transmission" OR compartmental OR metapopulation OR "meta-population" OR "state-space"}
\item
  \textbf{B (method):}
  \texttt{"particle filter" OR "sequential Monte Carlo" OR SMC OR "ensemble Kalman" OR "Kalman filter" OR "data assimilation" OR "partially observed Markov" OR Bayesian OR "Markov chain Monte Carlo" OR MCMC OR "approximate Bayesian computation" OR ABC OR "uncertainty quantification" OR calibration OR inference}
\item
  \textbf{C (artifact):}
  \texttt{software OR package OR library OR toolkit OR "R package" OR "Python package"}
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Inclusion and exclusion criteria.} A package is retained if it (1) is a released,
installable package or library, not a one-off analysis script; (2) simulates or fits
mechanistic compartmental or state-space epidemic models; (3) provides at least one built-in
method for Bayesian inference or uncertainty quantification (e.g., particle filter, ensemble
Kalman filter, particle MCMC, SMC\textsuperscript{2}, MCMC/HMC, ABC or nested sampling); (4)
is available as of the verification date; and (5) has English documentation. We exclude (a)
agent-based or simulation-only tools with no built-in inference (e.g., Covasim, MetaWards,
GLEAM, Starsim); (b) estimators of the reproduction number without a general forecasting engine
(e.g., EpiEstim, EpiNow2); (c) general-purpose statistical or probabilistic-programming
toolkits without epidemiological structure (e.g., Stan, PyMC, Pyro); and (d) packages with no
release or repository activity in the preceding five years. The boundary between a package we
compare and one we note as context is therefore epidemiological focus, not inference method: an
epidemic-specific package qualifies whether it calibrates by MCMC, ABC or a filter, whereas a
general Bayesian toolkit does not.

\textbf{Screening.} Screening proceeds in three stages. (1) \emph{Keyword pre-filter.} Each
record's title, abstract and keywords are scored for the co-occurrence of three term sets: a
\emph{software} set (package, software, toolkit, toolbox, library, framework, open-source,
freely available, available at/from, GitHub/CRAN/PyPI, implemented in, R package, Python
package, Julia, ``we present/introduce/develop''), an \emph{inference} set (particle filter,
sequential Monte Carlo, SMC, ensemble Kalman, Kalman filter, data assimilation, Bayesian,
Markov chain Monte Carlo, MCMC/pMCMC, approximate Bayesian, ABC-SMC, state-space, nested sampling, Hamiltonian Monte
Carlo, uncertainty quantification, quantified uncertainty, emulation, history matching,
iterated filtering, parameter estimation, calibration, likelihood), and an
\emph{epidemiology} set (epidemic, compartmental, SEIR, SIR model, metapopulation,
transmission, infectious disease, outbreak, forecast, and the major named pathogens:
influenza, COVID, dengue, malaria, measles, mpox, Ebola, pandemic). Records matching all
three sets advance to manual review; the remainder are set aside. The same term sets are
applied to both databases, and the pipeline verifies that this paragraph lists every term the
screen runs on. (2) \emph{Recall audit.} The set-aside records are audited three ways: records
with software and epidemiology terms but no inference term (``near misses'') are re-examined;
every set-aside record whose title carries a software-name cue together with an epidemiology
term is reviewed; and a seeded random sample of the remainder is reviewed to bound the miss
rate there. (3) \emph{Manual screening and verification.} Advanced and recovered records are
screened on title and abstract against the criteria, by rule where the disposition is
unambiguous (the record names a package already in the register, or falls squarely under an
exclusion criterion) and individually otherwise. Each retained package's capabilities are then
verified against its primary documentation (package website, CRAN or PyPI page, or software
paper), with a second author spot-checking the results. Every disposition and its reason is
recorded per record.

\textbf{Database results.} The PubMed query returned \textbf{2,403 records}, of which
\textbf{2,364 carried abstracts} and were screened. The pre-filter advanced \textbf{437
records}; \textbf{1,927} were set aside. Most advanced records were out of scope on
inspection: clinical prediction models, network meta-analyses, cost-effectiveness studies,
phylogenetic and genomic pipelines, pharmacokinetic software, and disease-mapping or nowcasting
utilities. Others were applications of packages already in the register. The PubMed screen
yielded one new package meeting the criteria, summer2/estival~\citep{trauer_data_2025}. It was
found through the inference terms \emph{calibration} and \emph{parameter estimation}: its
source paper never names a filter, so a vocabulary centred on filtering cannot see it.

The Lens.org export returned \textbf{1,009 records} (663 journal articles, 298 preprints and
48 conference papers). De-duplication against PubMed removed \textbf{340} overlaps,
leaving \textbf{669 novel records}, of which the pre-filter advanced \textbf{208}. Manual review
recovered packages already in the register (EpiILM, Pathogen.jl, Bernadette, spatPomp, PyRoss,
epidemia, hmer, MEmilio and ParticleDA.jl) and yielded one new package meeting the criteria,
MetaRVM~\citep{fadikar_developing_2025}. MetaRVM exists only as a preprint, and several other packages in this survey first appeared
as preprints (spatPomp, PyRoss, epidemia, LibBi, MEmilio), so including preprints was
necessary for a survey of software.

\textbf{Recall audit.} The 1,927 PubMed and 461 Lens.org records set aside
(\textbf{2,388} in total) were audited three ways. First, the near-miss stratum is empty: no
set-aside record carries software and epidemiology terms without an inference term, because the
inference set already includes vocabulary such as ``uncertainty quantification'',
``parameter estimation'' and ``calibration''. Second, every set-aside record whose title
carried a software-name cue together with an epidemiology term was reviewed: \textbf{101
records}, all of which proved out of scope or already in the register. Third, from the
\textbf{1,585} set-aside records carrying no software-name cue in the title, a
seeded random sample of \textbf{100} (seed 20260724) was reviewed, again finding no in-scope
package. Zero misses in 100 gives a rule-of-three upper 95\% bound of about 3\% on that
stratum.

\textbf{Outcome.} Screening 3,033 unique records under one lexicon, advancing \textbf{645} to
manual review, added two packages to the comparison (summer2/estival from PubMed and MetaRVM
from the Lens.org preprints). Beyond those two, the database and discovery sources converged on
the same packages. In all, the survey retained 13 packages for comparison with epymorph: the
11 in Table~1, and LibBi/rbi and epidemia, which were assessed on the same criteria but are not
tabulated (\S\ref{sec:S1-summaries}). A further 22 tools are summarized as context
(Table~\ref{tab:S1-context}). Excluded were agent-based simulators (Starsim, UVA-EpiHiper;
criterion a), reproduction-number estimators (EpiEstim, EpiNow2, EpiLPS; criterion b),
general-purpose toolkits (Stan, PyMC, Pyro, aghq; criterion c), and methods papers with no
released package.

\subsection{Criteria behind Table 1}\label{sec:S1-criteria}

In Table~1, \mkfull{} means the package provides a dedicated API, construct or data service
for the capability, and \mknone{} means it does not. Every cell is binary. A mark is never a
claim that a user could implement the capability, since every package surveyed is
programmable; where a package achieves something through general-purpose machinery rather than
a dedicated construct, it scores \mknone{}, and the closest such calls are argued below.

\textbf{Criteria.} The data rows require retrieval from a source, or data shipped for reuse as
model inputs; a data set that exists only to run the package's own examples does not count.
\emph{Model specification language} requires a domain-specific language or a declarative form
for compartments and transitions. \emph{Ready-made compartmental models} requires model objects
the user instantiates, not worked examples to copy. \emph{Operator for demographic
stratification} requires a construct that stratifies a model, not an array dimension the
modeler can use that way. \emph{Movement-based coupling} requires that individuals or infection
pressure are transported between units by a modeled flow, not that units are statistically
correlated. \emph{Forecast generation} requires a facility for producing forecasts from a
fitted model.

\textbf{Inference methods offered.} epymorph: PF, EnKF. FlepiMoP: MCMC, EMCEE ensemble.
spatPomp: PF (GIRF, block, ABF), EnKF, iterated filtering. pomp: PF, EnKF/EAKF, iterated
filtering, particle MCMC, ABC, Bayesian SMC. odin/dust/mcstate: PF, particle MCMC,
SMC\textsuperscript{2}, iterated filtering. LibBi/rbi: PF, particle MCMC,
SMC\textsuperscript{2}, extended Kalman. SimInf: ABC-SMC, PF, particle MCMC. PyRossGeo: MAP,
MCMC, nested sampling, density-tempered SMC for model evidence. surveillance (hhh4): penalized
likelihood. Epydemix: ABC (rejection, budget-constrained, ABC-SMC). summer2/estival: MCMC/HMC
and SMC via PyMC, gradient-free optimization via nevergrad. pypfilt/epifx: PF. epidemia:
Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (Stan). MetaRVM: Bayesian optimization.

\textbf{Borderline calls.} \emph{Movement-based coupling.} surveillance/hhh4 is the closest
call: it has inter-unit transmission terms and a dedicated coupling API (\texttt{neighbourhood}
weight matrices, \texttt{W\_powerlaw} with an estimated decay parameter), but the coupling is a
fitted regression weight and nothing is transported, so it scores \mknone{}. epidemia pools
\emph{parameters} across regions through multilevel priors, with no cross-region transmission
term, so its regions are statistically linked but epidemiologically independent. pomp is
single-population; spatPomp is its spatial extension. \emph{Demographic stratification.} Five
packages do real work through machinery that is not a stratification operator, and all five
score \mknone{}: odin and LibBi supply array dimensions with indexed equations (odin's
documentation works through age- and vaccination-structured examples, making it the strongest
of the five), SimInf ships age-stratified template models but cannot stratify anything else,
spatPomp separates the spatial index from the within-unit state vector so that strata could
occupy the latter, and epidemia's group structure can carry strata. \emph{Inference.} LibBi's
extended Kalman filter is a deterministic Kalman variant, not an ensemble Kalman filter, so its
EnKF cell is \mknone{}. PyRoss's \texttt{evidence\_smc} is a density-tempered SMC sampler over
parameter space for computing model evidence, not a sequential filter over latent states, and
its latent-state inference uses a Gaussian (system-size expansion) approximation; its
particle-filter cell is therefore \mknone{}. SimInf's three calibration methods
(\texttt{abc}, \texttt{pfilter}, \texttt{pmcmc}) postdate its published description and were
verified against the current package. \emph{Forecast evaluation.} Only surveillance/hhh4
(\texttt{scores}, \texttt{pit}, \texttt{calibrationTest}) and pypfilt/epifx (continuous ranked
probability scores) score forecasts in-package. epymorph and FlepiMoP both leave scoring to the
forecast hubs and their dedicated scoring packages, which we judge the right division of labor
rather than a gap, so forecast scoring is not a row of Table~1.

\subsection{Package summaries}\label{sec:S1-summaries}

Packages compared in Table~1 are grouped as in the table. Packages that were assessed but not
tabulated follow, and Table~\ref{tab:S1-context} lists the remaining tools.

\textbf{Pipelines.}

\begin{itemize}
\item
  \textbf{FlepiMoP} (Python and R): a meta-population compartmental pipeline configured in
  YAML, whose \texttt{compartments} and \texttt{seir: transitions} sections are a declarative
  model specification, with Bayesian inference by a custom MCMC with an EMCEE ensemble sampler
  and an integrated forecasting workflow. Its data layer ships US county populations, US
  commuting flows and global age-structure tables, and retrieves COVID-19 and influenza ground
  truth from the Delphi Epidata API and CDC FluSight. No particle or ensemble Kalman filter.
  The closest comparator to epymorph~\citep{lemaitre_flepimop_2024}.
\item
  \textbf{MetaRVM} (R): a meta-population compartmental package for subpopulations defined by
  geography, demographics or other strata, developed with the Chicago Department of Public
  Health. It calibrates by Bayesian optimization (a Gaussian-process surrogate with
  Thompson-sampling acquisition), applied in sequential phases as new weeks of data arrive.
  No particle filter, ensemble Kalman filter or MCMC, and no data
  acquisition~\citep{fadikar_developing_2025}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Toolkits.}

\begin{itemize}
\item
  \textbf{spatPomp} (R): the spatiotemporal extension of pomp for geographic meta-populations,
  with a guided intermediate-resampling filter, block particle filter, adapted bagged filter
  and ensemble Kalman filter, and parameter estimation by iterated filtering. Models are
  hand-coded, with no built-in epidemiological structures or data
  services~\citep{ionides_iterated_2024,asfaw_tutorial_2024}.
\item
  \textbf{pomp} (R): inference for partially observed Markov processes, with particle,
  ensemble Kalman and ensemble-adjustment Kalman filters, iterated filtering, particle MCMC,
  ABC and Bayesian SMC. Single-population; models are hand-coded as C snippets; no data
  acquisition~\citep{king_statistical_2016}.
\item
  \textbf{odin/dust/mcstate} (R): stochastic state-space models written in the odin
  domain-specific language, with particle filtering, particle MCMC, SMC\textsuperscript{2} and
  iterated filtering; no ensemble Kalman filter. Reorganized in 2024 as odin2/dust2/monty
  (dust2 runs the filters, monty succeeds mcstate)~\citep{fitzjohn_reproducible_2021}.
\item
  \textbf{pypfilt/epifx} (Python): a bootstrap particle filter for online estimation and
  forecasting (pypfilt) with an epidemic modeling layer (epifx) that provides mechanistic
  infection models and CRPS scoring; used operationally for Australian influenza and COVID-19
  forecasting. Single-population, with no ensemble Kalman filter or data
  acquisition~\citep{moss_pypfilt_2024}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Simulation tools.}

\begin{itemize}
\item
  \textbf{SimInf} (R): stochastic meta-population simulation by the Gillespie algorithm, with
  template models (SIR, SISe, SEIR), and calibration by ABC-SMC, a bootstrap particle filter
  and particle MCMC; not oriented toward sequential forecasting~\citep{widgren_siminf_2019}.
\item
  \textbf{PyRoss/PyRossGeo} (Python): Bayesian inference (MAP, MCMC, nested sampling) for age-
  and contact-structured compartmental models, with spatial commuting through the PyRossGeo
  companion. No particle or ensemble Kalman filter (see \S\ref{sec:S1-criteria}). Ships
  household, workplace, school and community contact matrices for several countries, which
  epymorph does not~\citep{adhikari_inference_2020}.
\item
  \textbf{surveillance (hhh4)} (R): endemic--epidemic regression for areal count time series
  with neighborhood coupling and one-step-ahead or simulated forecasts, fit by penalized
  likelihood; one of two surveyed packages that score forecasts
  in-package~\citep{meyer_spatio-temporal_2017,paul_predictive_2011}.
\item
  \textbf{Epydemix} (Python): single-population compartmental models with approximate Bayesian
  calibration (rejection, budget-constrained rejection, ABC-SMC). A companion repository
  supplies population structures and age-stratified contact matrices for more than 400 regions
  worldwide, the most extensive data layer of any comparator after
  FlepiMoP's~\citep{gozzi_epydemix_2025}.
\item
  \textbf{summer2/estival} (Python): a framework for constructing compartmental models whose
  defining feature is arbitrary stratification (age, strain, risk or user-defined), including
  mixing matrices and flows between strata, compiled through JAX. The companion package estival
  calibrates summer2 models through several backends behind one interface: PyMC (MCMC/HMC and
  SMC) and nevergrad (gradient-free optimization). The closest comparator to epymorph on modular
  model construction with swappable inference, but with no particle or ensemble Kalman filter,
  no movement model and no data acquisition~\citep{trauer_data_2025}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Assessed but not tabulated.} These two packages meet the criteria and were assessed on
the same rows, but adding them would lengthen Table~1 without changing the comparison.

\begin{itemize}
\item
  \textbf{LibBi/rbi} (C++/CUDA with an R interface): a state-space modeling language with
  particle filtering, particle MCMC, SMC\textsuperscript{2} and an extended Kalman filter,
  parallelized for GPUs and clusters. A general state-space engine whose epidemiological use is
  through \texttt{rbi.helpers}; development has slowed~\citep{murray_bayesian_2015}.
\item
  \textbf{epidemia} (R): semi-mechanistic Bayesian modeling with renewal-equation dynamics, fit
  by Hamiltonian Monte Carlo through Stan, estimating reproduction numbers, latent infections
  and intervention effects. A renewal model rather than a compartmental one; supports regional
  partial pooling but not movement-based coupling~\citep{scott_epidemia_2021}.
\end{itemize}

General-purpose filtering and probabilistic-programming libraries (\texttt{particles},

\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:S1-limitations}

This survey is a snapshot, and package capabilities change. Re-verification changed seven
marks between June and September 2026, most of them after we read each primary reference in
full rather than in abstract, and several changed in a comparator's favour: pomp gained the
ensemble Kalman filter, SimInf the particle filter and two further calibration methods, FlepiMoP
automated surveillance acquisition and declarative model specification, and surveillance/hhh4
forecast scoring, while PyRossGeo lost the particle filter. The PyRossGeo case shows a hazard
of feature comparisons built from vocabulary: a routine named for SMC\textsuperscript{2} turned
out not to filter latent states, so we verified each filtering mark against the function's
documented behaviour rather than its name.

Binary marks sharpen the comparison but flatten real differences in strength: odin's array
dimensions and pomp's hand-written C snippets both score \mknone{} for demographic
stratification, though the first does much more of the work. \S\ref{sec:S1-criteria} carries
those distinctions. Our criteria, centred on mechanistic state-space inference, deliberately
exclude agent-based and purely statistical forecasting tools that some readers may consider
adjacent. A keyword pre-filter can only find packages whose abstracts describe what they do:
summer2/estival was missed by an earlier vocabulary centred on filtering and recovered only
once the inference set included \emph{calibration} and \emph{parameter estimation}, which is
why we rely on the recall audit rather than the pre-filter. Finally, we did not search Scopus
or Web of Science; a package indexed in neither PubMed nor Lens.org could in principle be
missed, although the two databases and the discovery sources converged on the same packages.
